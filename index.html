A travel planning system that generates personalized, operationally feasible day-wise itineraries for tourists visiting Sri Lanka.

Problem

The research problem

Tourists visiting Sri Lanka frequently struggle to plan efficient and realistic day-to-day itineraries.

The people affected are international and domestic tourists, especially independent ones who rely on digital tools.

Existing travel planning apps are largely generic and globally oriented. They do not account for five factors specific to Sri Lanka:

  • Attraction opening hours
  • Travel distances
  • Visit durations
  • Traffic conditions
  • Route feasibility

So tourists assemble itineraries by hand from fragmented sources. The process is time-consuming, stressful and error-prone.

Impact

Why it matters

Poor itinerary planning has effects in five dimensions.

  • Social

    Poor planning reduces travel satisfaction: attractions are missed and time is used inefficiently.

  • Technical

    There is a gap in decision-support technology tailored to developing tourism contexts.

  • Economic

    Tourist spending becomes less efficient, with a negative effect on small tourism operators.

  • Environmental

    Poorly optimized routes increase unnecessary travel and fuel consumption.

  • Ethical

    Without accessible planning tools, first-time and budget-conscious tourists are at a disadvantage.

Prior work

Existing systems

Commercial platforms

Google Travel, TripAdvisor, Sygic Travel, Expedia Trip Planners

What they do

They rely on globally aggregated datasets and offer limited personalization beyond broad preference selection.

Where they fall short

They do not adequately consider attraction-specific opening hours, realistic visit durations, regional travel conditions or route feasibility in developing tourism contexts. The itineraries they generate often lack practicality and need significant manual adjustment.

Academic research

Personalized itinerary recommendation, route optimization, tourism recommender systems

What it does

It uses rule-based planning, heuristics and evolutionary algorithms. Multi-constraint optimization has been shown to improve travel efficiency and satisfaction.

Where it falls short

Most of it targets data-rich, urban or Western tourism environments, and assumes continuous API access, extensive datasets and stable infrastructure. Many proposed models remain conceptual or simulation-level and are never implemented as functional systems evaluated through real user interaction.

Research gap

Three gaps in existing work

  1. Gap 1

    Localization

    There is a lack of travel planning systems focused on Sri Lanka or other developing countries that integrate localized tourism data and real-world constraints. Commercial platforms draw on globally aggregated data. Academic work mostly assumes data-rich environments with continuous API access.

  2. Gap 2

    Integration

    Existing systems treat personalization and feasibility separately. They do not balance the two dynamically within a single itinerary engine. The itineraries they generate often lack practicality and need significant manual adjustment.

  3. Gap 3

    Evaluation

    Formal usability and effectiveness evaluation of generated itineraries, from the user’s perspective, has received limited attention. Many proposed models remain conceptual or simulation-level and are never evaluated as working systems with real users.

Together, these point to the need for a lightweight, localized, user-centered smart travel planning system that combines personalization, constraint-aware optimization and practical evaluation in a real-world tourism context.

The evaluation results reported on this site are computed on synthetic data. They do not include an evaluation with real users.

Approach

The proposed solution

The proposal follows an Input–Process–Output model.

Input

What the planner is given

From the tourist

  • Interests and attraction preferences
  • Budget level
  • Available travel time and trip duration
  • Preferred travel pace
  • Starting location

From a local tourism knowledge base

  • Points of interest
  • Operating hours
  • Average visit durations
  • Geographic coordinates
Process

How the itinerary is built

An automated itinerary generation and optimization engine, applying a hybrid of two techniques:

Rule-based logic

Enforces feasibility constraints such as opening hours and time windows.

Heuristic optimization

Improves route ordering and reduces travel time.

Output

What the tourist receives

Personalized day-wise itineraries, with:

  • Ordered attractions per day
  • Estimated arrival and departure times
  • Travel durations between locations
  • Visual route representations on an interactive map
Implementation

What we built

The system runs as separate processes, not a monolith. The proposal describes an Input–Process–Output flow; the built system is organized as a hub with three sibling services.

Design property

The browser only talks to the gateway

The browser only ever talks to the gateway. The gateway only ever talks to the planner. The planner fans out to the three domain services.

Design property

The system degrades visibly

If one service is unreachable, the plan is still returned, with a banner naming which part used a fallback.

Why the town tier exists

The CT tier exists because 129 curated localities and hubs could not cover a real trip. Someone starting from Aluthgama had no way to say so.

Why coordinates are medians

Each town’s coordinate is the median of the POIs assigned to it, so one mis-geocoded row cannot drag a town into the sea.
