Sub-objective

What this component sets out to do

To suggest the best mode of transport between attractions based on distance, time, cost and convenience, ensuring efficient and practical travel during multi-day trips.

Sub-objective, Topic Assessment Form

Tasks

  1. Calculate distances and estimated travel times between attractions.
  2. Gather typical transport options and their cost and time metrics.
  3. Recommend the optimal mode for each trip segment using simple rules.
  4. Test recommendations on sample itineraries.
Novelty

The novelty claim

Unlike planners that assume a single travel mode, this module provides adaptive transport recommendations, improving travel efficiency and convenience without requiring complex AI or optimization.

Novelty, Topic Assessment Form

What it deliberately does not claim

It does not claim complex AI, and it does not claim optimization. The engine is rule-based: there is no training and there are no fitted coefficients. Every weight is declared and every elimination carries a stated reason. That restraint is the claim.

Data foundation

The route audit

smart_travel_transport_route_v1_0_reanchored.csv: 15,336 rows, 2,556 origin-destination pairs, 72 distinct attractions.

Source: reports/route_audit.md

A data problem found and fixed: duplicate modes

Every pair has 6 rows but only 3–4 distinct modes. All 2,556 pairs contain the same mode more than once, with different time and cost estimates for an identical leg. The median time spread between duplicates is 8 minutes; the maximum is 99.

Why it had to be resolved. Treating duplicates as independent candidates would let one mode occupy two ranking positions.

The fix. Take the median per mode, and carry the spread as uncertainty.

Each factor multiplies the dataset distance. The direction is not uniform: on the coast (+5.94%) and in towns (+7.93%) the dataset overstates real road distance, so those factors are below 1. In hill country (−11.19%), rural lowland (−11.39%) and rolling terrain (−6.13%) it understates, so those are above 1. The −2.87% median is the overall figure, not a per-terrain one.

The factors and the error percentages are two different medians over the same pairs: each factor is the median of osrm_km / dataset_km for that terrain, while the error is the median of the per-pair percentage errors. A median of ratios is not the reciprocal of a median of errors, so the two columns do not invert each other exactly.

The point

The component did not trust its own synthetic distances

It measured them against real road routing and corrected them per terrain. That is the strongest methodological claim on this page.

Method

The four-stage scoring engine

Rule-based multi-criteria ranking. No training, no fitted coefficients: every weight is declared and every elimination carries a stated reason.

Source: reports/scoring_engine.md

The order of the stages is the design argument

  1. OptionsThe candidate modes for the leg.
  2. Hard feasibility filterRemoves modes that break a rule, each with a stated reason.
  3. Criteria scoringScores the remaining modes under a declared weight profile.
  4. Stated preferenceAdds 0.15 to a preferred mode’s score.

A preference can move a ranking it cannot decide, and cannot return a mode the filter removed.

Feasibility rules

A mode is eliminated, with the reason stated, when:

  1. The leg is outside its operating range.
  2. Its capacity is below the party size.
  3. The tourist requires wheelchair access and the mode is unsuitable.
  4. The leg is longer or slower than the stated maximum.
  5. It exceeds a cost or transfer ceiling.
  6. The route data marks it infeasible.
  7. It is a fully exposed mode in heavy rain.
Design principle

Unavailable, never omitted

A mode outside its band is returned marked unavailable, with the reason. It is never left out, because “a response that lists only the winner cannot be challenged.”

Stated preference

A 0.15 boost, never a filter

A stated preferred mode adds 0.15 to the score it already earned. The boost is applied after the criteria, never as a filter.

Stated limitations

Stated limitations

Limitations of the calibration

  1. Sample size. 54 of 2,556 pairs were checked; the public OSRM server is rate limited.
  2. Driving only. Tuk-tuk, van, taxi and ride-hailing have no OSRM profile, so their times remain derived from speed assumptions.
  3. Free traffic conditions. OSRM assumes unimpeded travel. Real Sri Lankan drive times, especially around Colombo, are longer at peak.
  4. Coordinate quality. Only pairs with reliable registry coordinates were used.

Source: reports/calibration.md

Limitations of the scoring engine

  1. No accuracy evaluation exists. The fourth task, testing recommendations on sample itineraries, has no result. An expert-panel evaluation against ground truth is designed (transport/scripts/expert_evaluation.py) but has not been run: the ground-truth labels have not been collected. Whether the engine chooses the right mode is not established.
  2. The weights are declared, not fitted. That is deliberate and makes every decision inspectable. It also means no weight profile has been validated against observed tourist behaviour.
  3. One mode almost always wins. Private car is chosen for 2,515 of 2,554 pairs under the balanced profile: 98.5%. A rule set that returns one answer almost always is hard to distinguish from a constant.
  4. The feasibility filter rarely binds. Mean candidate modes are 3.36 against 3.34 feasible: about 0.02 modes removed per pair. It eliminated every candidate for only two pairs. The filter is central to the design argument but does little work on this dataset.
  5. Only driving is validated. OSRM has no profile for tuk-tuk, van, taxi or ride-hailing, and bus, train, motorcycle and walking are not validated either. Private car is effectively the only calibrated mode.
